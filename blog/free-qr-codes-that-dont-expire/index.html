Free codes that never expire

Yes, they exist — they're called static QR codes. Here's how to tell them apart from the trial-trap kind, and how to make yours in 30 seconds.

2026-10-14 · 4 min read

Static vs. dynamic: the only distinction that matters

A static code encodes your link directly in the pattern. Nothing to expire, nothing to renew — it works as long as the link works. A dynamic code encodes a redirect on the vendor's server, so they can charge you monthly to keep it alive. The full explainer: static vs dynamic QR codes.

The trial trap

Many "free QR code generators" quietly create dynamic codes on a short trial. Your printed flyers work beautifully — until the trial ends and every code goes dead. Red flags: the site asks for your email before generating, mentions "scan analytics", or talks about "campaigns". A truly free static generator needs none of that.

The 30-second test

Before you trust a generator with your print run: generate a code without creating an account. If it works, it's static. Static codes from FreeQrly need no account, so there's nothing to expire. That's also why QR codes don't have to expire.

What can still break (it's not the code)

A static code lasts forever, but its destination might not: if you delete the page it links to, the code "stops working". Link to stable URLs you control, and your codes will outlive most of your marketing materials.

Good to know

Do free QR codes expire?

Static ones don't — the destination is baked into the pattern. Dynamic ones from trial-based generators stop working when the subscription or trial ends.

How can I tell if my QR code will expire?

If the generator required an account or email, it's almost certainly dynamic and tied to a subscription. True static generators work with no sign-up.
