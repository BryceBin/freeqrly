How FreeQrly Stays Free — and What's the Catch?

There is no catch. That is the short answer, and it is also the whole point of this article.

November 19, 2026 · 17 min read

FreeQrly is a free QR code generator. The free tier is sustained by Pro subscriptions — a small share of users who need more than 20 dynamic codes pay $9 a month or $90 a year, and that money pays for hosting and development. During the current limited-time offer, free dynamic codes are unlimited; after the offer ends, the free plan includes 20 dynamic codes. No advertising on the site, no selling of user data, no charge for static codes, and no expiry timer designed to force a renewal.

If you have used free tools for any length of time, you have good reason to be skeptical. "Free" usually means one of three things: free until you need the feature that matters, free in exchange for your data, or free until the code on your printed flyer quietly stops working. This article explains exactly what is free, what Pro is, how the service pays its bills, and what it does not do — so you can judge the model for yourself.

What Is Free, Exactly?

Start with the part that matters most: static codes are free, with no sign-up, no watermark, and no expiry date. You open the generator, type in a link or choose one of the 17 QR code types, customize the look, download the code, and you are done. No account, no email address, no ad before the download, and no logo stamped across the code that you can only remove by paying.

A static code is also generated entirely in your browser. Nothing you type is uploaded to make the code, and nothing about the code is tracked. The destination — a website address, a Wi-Fi password, a piece of text — is encoded directly into the pattern of squares. Once downloaded, the code is yours. It does not check in with a server when scanned, and it does not stop working because a subscription lapsed. Printed on a business card today, it will still scan years from now, as long as its destination still exists.

Dynamic codes are the second half of the free tier, and they work differently. A dynamic code points to a short redirect that FreeQrly hosts, and that redirect can be changed after the code is printed. That is what makes dynamic codes useful — and why they need an account, explained in the FAQ below.

On the free tier, dynamic codes include:

  • Free sign-in to create them. You create a free account and can create dynamic codes at no cost.
  • Editing after printing. If the link behind a printed code changes — a new menu, a corrected URL — you can update the destination without reprinting.
  • Scan statistics. You can see how often a dynamic code has been scanned.
  • Unlimited scans. No cap on how many times a dynamic code can be scanned, free or Pro.
  • 20 dynamic codes. The free tier allows up to 20 dynamic codes.

That last number is the one real limit on the free tier. Twenty dynamic codes is enough for a small business with a handful of menus and posters, a teacher with a set of classroom links, or a freelancer with a few campaigns. It is not enough for an agency managing codes for dozens of clients, or a retailer with a code on every product line. That gap — between 20 and unlimited — is where Pro lives, and it is the only place Pro lives.

Everything else sits outside the free-versus-paid question. Batch generation, adding a logo in the center of a code, and the choice of 17 QR code types are part of the generator itself, not features held back for paying users. The site is available in 10 languages. None of that changes depending on whether you pay.

What Pro Is — and What It Is Not

Pro costs $9 a month, or $90 a year if you pay annually — twelve months for the price of ten. There is no separate annual-only feature, no onboarding call, no priority support tier, and no "Pro-only" code style.

Its only difference from the free tier is unlimited dynamic codes. Pro adds no other features. It does not remove ads, because there are no ads to remove. It does not unlock scan statistics or editing after printing, because both are already included for free.

If you only need static codes, Pro will never be relevant to you, and that is fine. You can use FreeQrly for years, generate hundreds of static codes, and never see a paywall, a trial countdown, or an email urging you to upgrade. Static codes are not a free trial of a paid product. They are the product, free, with no time limit.

If you need dynamic codes, count how many. If the answer is 20 or fewer, the free tier covers you. If it is more than 20, Pro removes the cap and nothing else changes. You do not get a different dashboard or a different experience. You get more of the same thing.

Many free tools make pricing hard to understand on purpose: a free tier with five limits, a Pro tier with three more, an enterprise tier you have to email about, and a comparison table where the checkmarks do not quite line up. The confusion is part of the sales funnel — if you cannot tell what is free, you are more likely to pay just to be safe. FreeQrly's pricing is one line long because the decision behind it is one line long: charge the people who need a lot of dynamic codes, and leave everyone else alone.

How the Business Sustains Itself

A free service still has bills. Servers, domain names, and development time all cost money. A tool that pretends otherwise is either spending someone else's money or planning to charge you later in a way it has not told you about yet. So the honest question is not "how can this possibly be free?" but "who pays, and for what?"

The answer is: Pro subscribers pay, and they pay for hosting and development.

That is the entire revenue model. There is no advertising on the site — no banner ads, no sponsored results, no "recommended" styles that are actually paid placements. There is no sale of user data, because there is nothing to sell: static codes are generated in your browser and never uploaded, and the data attached to a free account is the minimum needed to run dynamic codes — your login, your codes, and their scan counts. There is no charge for static codes, and no expiry mechanism that would let the service hold a printed code hostage until you renew.

This model works because costs stay proportionate to revenue. Static codes are cheap to serve by design: they are generated in your browser, so FreeQrly's servers do not have to create, store, or track them. The part that needs servers and maintenance is the dynamic code system — the redirects, the editing, the statistics — precisely the part with a limit on the free tier and an unlimited option on Pro. The people who use the most server resources are the people who pay. Static-code users pay nothing, because they cost almost nothing to serve.

Be candid about the limits, too. This model does not make FreeQrly a large company, and it is not trying to. A one-feature revenue stream will never produce the growth venture-backed competitors chase — a trade-off, not an oversight. The upside is that incentives point in the right direction. When your only revenue comes from people who voluntarily pay for unlimited dynamic codes, you do not need to squeeze the free tier — no ads, no invented limit, no charging for something that used to be free. The free tier is not a loss leader tolerated while users convert; it is the product most people use, and Pro exists to support it.

That is also why the pricing has stayed simple. A model with one paid tier and one difference is easy to explain, easy to trust, and hard to quietly change. If Pro ever gained a second difference, it would be visible immediately, because there is only one line to check. This article is a record of that line as it stands today: Pro is unlimited dynamic codes, and nothing else.

What FreeQrly Does NOT Do

Skepticism about free tools is usually well earned — the traps are real and common. Here they are, named plainly, so you can check FreeQrly against each one.

No watermark on free codes. Some free QR code tools stamp their logo across the middle of a perfectly good code, and the only way to remove it is to pay. FreeQrly does not do this. A free static code is a clean code. You can add your own logo in the center if you want to — that is your design choice.

No forced account for static codes. Some tools require an email address before they will generate anything at all, even a one-off code for a Wi-Fi password. The account is not there because the feature needs it; it is there because an email address is valuable for marketing. FreeQrly does not require an account for static codes, because a static code does not need one. There is nothing to save, nothing to sync, and no reason to know who you are.

No expiry. Some tools give you a free code that works for 30 days, or 90 days, or a year — and then stops, usually just after you have printed it on five hundred flyers. The expiry is not a technical necessity; it is a renewal mechanism. FreeQrly codes do not expire. A static code cannot expire, because no server is involved in scanning it. A dynamic code does not expire either, free or paid. If you never pay, or if you cancel Pro, your codes keep working — see the FAQ below for what changes and what does not.

No scan limits. Some tools cap the number of times a free code can be scanned, then either stop working or demand payment to keep going. A scan limit means a printed code can stop working after a set number of scans. FreeQrly has no scan limits. A dynamic code on the free tier can be scanned an unlimited number of times. The limit is on how many dynamic codes you can create, not on how much they can be used.

No selling user data. Some free tools are free because the user is the product: usage data and scan behavior are packaged and sold, or used to serve targeted advertising. FreeQrly does not sell user data. There is no advertising on the site to target in the first place, and static codes produce no user data at all, because they never leave your browser.

Who Pays and Who Doesn't

Most people who use FreeQrly never pay anything. That is not a failure of the business model; it is the business model. The free tier is designed to be complete for the most common uses — a static code for a website, a menu, a business card, a classroom handout — and most users' needs stop there.

The people who pay are the small share who need many dynamic codes: those managing campaigns across many locations, updating destinations regularly, and tracking scans across dozens of codes. They pay because the cap of 20 dynamic codes is a real constraint for them, not because a feature they need is locked away. The decision to pay is practical — "I need more than 20 dynamic codes" — not a reaction to a countdown timer or a watermark.

No percentage is given here, because there is no verified figure to give. Any article that tells you "only 3% of users pay" without showing how it reached that number is asking you to trust a number it has not earned. The honest statement is qualitative: most users never pay, a small share do, and the service is built so that the small share's payments cover the costs of serving everyone.

That structure also explains why the free tier will not be quietly hollowed out. If it stopped being useful, most users would leave — and Pro subscribers are drawn from those same users, people who started free and grew past 20 dynamic codes. Making the free tier worse would shrink the pool of future Pro subscribers. Keep the free tier genuinely good, and the paid tier will find the people who genuinely need it.

Why Dynamic Codes Need an Account at All

"Free sign-in" can sound like a contradiction. If static codes need no account, why do dynamic codes need one?

The answer is technical, not commercial. A static code contains its destination. A dynamic code contains a pointer to a redirect that FreeQrly hosts, and that redirect has to live somewhere, belong to someone, and be editable only by its owner. Without an account, FreeQrly could not tell who may change a code's destination, show you only your own codes and scan statistics, or stop one stranger from editing another stranger's printed poster.

The account exists to make the dynamic feature work: to hold your list of codes, protect your ability to edit them, and count their scans. It is not there to collect an email address for marketing, or as a stepping stone to a forced upgrade. The 20-code limit is the only commercial element, stated up front.

If you do not need editing or statistics, you do not need an account at all. Use a static code. That is the honest recommendation, and one a sales-driven tool would never make.

The Catch, Revisited

So, what's the catch?

There is not one in the sense most people mean — no hidden charge, no expiring code, no data sale, no ad. There is a trade-off, and it is worth naming honestly, because every free service has one.

The trade-off is that FreeQrly is a small, focused tool with one paid tier, not a large platform with a large team. Development moves at the pace Pro subscriptions can fund. If you need an enterprise contract, a dedicated account manager, or a service-level agreement with guaranteed uptime penalties, this is not that product, and it does not pretend to be.

What you get in exchange is a straightforward deal, stated in one paragraph: static codes are free forever, with no sign-up and no expiry; dynamic codes are free up to 20, with editing and statistics included; Pro is $9 a month or $90 a year and removes only the 20-code cap; nothing is advertised to you and nothing about you is sold.

That is a modest promise. It is also a checkable one. Generate a static code without signing up. Print it. Scan it in a year. Create a free account, make a dynamic code, edit its destination, and watch the scan count. Cancel Pro if you ever try it — your codes keep working. The model either holds up under that kind of checking, or it does not.

Good to know

Will static codes stay free?

Yes. Static codes are free with no sign-up, no watermark, and no expiry — not a promotional period and not a trial. There is no plan to charge for them. The promise is structural: static codes are generated in your browser and cost almost nothing to serve. Pro pays for the dynamic-code system, not for static codes. Static codes remain free regardless of whether you ever pay.

Will my codes expire if I never pay?

No. Codes do not expire — static or dynamic, free or paid. A static code cannot expire, because no server is involved when someone scans it; the destination is encoded in the pattern itself. A dynamic code does not expire either. If you never pay, your codes keep working for as long as their destinations exist. There is no renewal date, no grace period, and no "your code will stop working in 7 days" email.

Do you sell my data?

No. FreeQrly does not sell user data, and there is no advertising on the site. Static codes generate no user data at all, because they are created entirely in your browser — nothing you type is uploaded, and nothing about the code is tracked. For dynamic codes, the data attached to your account is the minimum needed to run the feature: your login, your codes, their destinations, and their scan counts. That data exists so you can edit your codes and see their statistics. It is not sold or shared with advertisers.

Why do you need an account for dynamic codes at all?

Because a dynamic code is not a self-contained image — it is a pointer to a redirect hosted by FreeQrly, and that redirect needs an owner. The account ties a code to you: it lets you see your codes, change their destinations after printing, and view scan statistics, and it stops anyone else from editing them. Without an account, there would be no way to prove a code is yours. Static codes need no account because they need no server — the destination is baked into the code itself.

What happens if I cancel Pro?

Your codes keep working. Cancelling Pro does not delete your codes, expire them, or stop them scanning. What changes is the cap: your account returns to the free tier's limit of 20 dynamic codes. You keep the codes you already created and they continue to work — they can still be scanned an unlimited number of times, edited, and tracked — but you cannot create new dynamic codes until the number you hold is back within the free allowance. There is no penalty for cancelling, no reactivation fee, and no countdown during which printed codes stop working. If you need unlimited dynamic codes again later, you can resubscribe; if you do not, the free tier is still there.
