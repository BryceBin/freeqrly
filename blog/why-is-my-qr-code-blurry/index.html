Why is my QR code blurry?

A blurry QR code is almost always a resolution problem: too few pixels stretched across too much space. The fix is to export bigger — or as SVG.

October 17, 2026 · 4 min read

A QR code looks sharp on screen and turns to mush on paper. The cause is nearly always the same: the image did not have enough pixels for the size it was printed at. A QR code is a grid of tiny squares — when there are not enough pixels, the edges smear, phones cannot find the grid, and scans fail.

The 5 usual causes

  1. Low-resolution export. A 512 px PNG looks fine at 2 cm wide. Blown up to a 20 cm poster, each module becomes a smudge. The bigger the print, the more pixels you need.
  2. JPEG compression. JPEG smears sharp edges — exactly what a QR code is made of. A code saved as a low-quality JPEG can fail even at small sizes. PNG or SVG only.
  3. Stretching. Dragging a corner of the image distorts the grid: squares become rectangles, and scanners give up. Always resize proportionally.
  4. Bad print. Low-DPI printers, ink bleed on rough or absorbent paper, and glossy glare all soften edges. Matte paper and a laser printer beat an inkjet on a napkin.
  5. Copy of a copy. Screenshotting a code from a message, then screenshotting that screenshot, loses resolution each time. Always go back to the original export.

How to fix it

  • Export bigger. PNG at 1024 px for cards and flyers, 2048 px for posters. Rule of thumb: the print size in cm, times ~100, is the pixels you want.
  • Use SVG for print. SVG is vector — infinitely scalable with zero blur. Use it whenever the printer or designer accepts it.
  • Keep proportions. Hold Shift (or the lock icon) when resizing so the squares stay square.
  • Test at final size. Print one copy at the real size, step back to the real viewing distance, and scan it with your phone. If it reads first try, the batch is safe.

Good to know

Why does my QR code look blurry but still scan?

Phones are forgiving — error correction recovers a surprising amount of blur. But a code that scans in your bright office may fail in dim light or at arm's length. Sharp is safe; barely-scanning is not.

What resolution do I need for a poster?

2048 px PNG minimum, or SVG. A poster viewed from 2 meters needs far more pixels than a business card viewed from 30 cm — see the print size guide for the distance rule.

PNG or SVG for print?

SVG whenever the printer or designer accepts it — it scales infinitely with zero blur. PNG at 1024–2048 px is the fallback for office printing and apps that don't take SVG.

Can I fix a blurry printed code without reprinting?

No. The blur is baked into the printed image. Reprint from a higher-resolution export (or SVG). Editing a dynamic code won't help — the pattern on paper is what it is.
