Printable table cards for WiFi, reviews & menus

Print one card, stand it on the table, and let guests scan instead of asking. This template gives you three ready-made cards — pick the one you need, drop in your own QR code, and print.

November 4, 2026 · 4 min read

Table cards work because guests are already sitting down with a phone in hand. A short line of text and one clear QR code is all it takes — no app to install, nothing to explain. This template covers the three requests cafés and restaurants hear most: the WiFi password, a review, and the menu.

Each card below is 105 × 148 mm (A6), so it fits a standard table stand or a folded card holder. To use it: generate your QR code, paste it into the dashed slot on the card you want, print on card stock, and cut along the edge. Print only the card you need — there is no need to print all three. If your WiFi details might change, see how to share WiFi with a QR code; for the review card, getting Google reviews with a QR code covers linking straight to your review page. More ideas for using codes in a dining room are in QR codes for restaurants, and run through the pre-print checklist before a big print run.

Tip: use a dynamic QR code for the menu card if your menu changes. That way the printed card never needs reprinting — see how to edit a QR code after printing.

Good to know

What paper should I print table cards on?

Plain paper works for a quick test, but card stock (around 250–300 gsm) stands up in a holder and survives a busy service. If the card will sit near drinks, laminate it — and then test-scan it, since gloss can glare under spotlights.

Can I print these in black and white?

Yes. The cards are designed so the text and QR slot read fine without color; the colored headers just print as grey. What matters is contrast: a dark code on a light background, never the reverse.

How do I put my own QR code in the dashed slot?

Generate your code, print it at 30 × 30 mm, and paste it over the dashed box in your layout tool — or print the card, then stick a printed code label over the slot. Either way, test-scan the finished card before printing the full run.

What size should the QR code be?

30 × 30 mm is sized for a guest scanning from their seat, roughly 30–50 cm away. Do not shrink it to squeeze in more text; a code that is too small for its distance is the most common reason a printed code fails.

Can I print just one of the three cards?

Yes — that is the point. Pick the card you need, print that page or crop to that card, and ignore the other two. Each card is a standalone 105 × 148 mm (A6) card.

My menu changes often. Do I have to reprint every time?

Not if you use a dynamic QR code for the menu card: the destination behind the code can be updated while the printed card stays the same. A WiFi card can stay static unless your password changes.
