How to scan a QR code

No app required. Every modern iPhone and Android phone scans QR codes with the camera it already has. Here's how — plus what to do when nothing happens.

2026-10-14 · 4 min read

  1. Point your camera at the code Open the built-in Camera app and point it at the QR code, keeping the whole square in frame.
  2. Tap the notification A banner or link preview appears — tap it to open the destination.
  3. On iPhone, use the built-in shortcuts Control Center QR scanner, or long-press the Camera icon. For a code inside a photo, long-press it in Photos.
  4. On Android, use Lens or Quick Settings Google Lens in the camera or Google app, or the Scan QR code tile in Quick Settings. For a saved image, Google Photos + Lens.
  5. If nothing happens, fix the code, not the phone Move closer, wipe the lens, and check the code itself: too small, low contrast, damaged, or printed on a curve.
  6. Check the preview before opening Glance at the URL before tapping, especially on codes you find in public places.

On iPhone

Open the Camera app and point it at the code — hold steady for a second. A yellow notification appears; tap it. That's it. This works on any modern iPhone — Apple's guide covers the Camera app and Code Scanner.

Two shortcuts worth knowing: open Control Center and tap the QR scanner icon (add it via Settings → Control Center if it's missing), or long-press the Camera icon on the lock screen. If the code is in a photo or screenshot, open it in the Photos app and long-press the code — iOS detects it right in the image.

On Android

Most Android phones scan from the built-in camera too: point, wait for the prompt, tap. On Pixel and many others, Google Lens in the camera or Google app handles it. Samsung phones have a dedicated scanner in the Quick Settings panel — swipe down and look for "Scan QR code". And like iPhone, you can open a saved image in Google Photos and tap the Lens button.

When nothing happens

Nine times out of ten it's the code, not your phone: too small, low contrast, damaged, or printed on a curve. There's a full checklist in why your QR code won't scan. Dirty camera lens is the classic overlooked one — wipe it and try again.

A 3-second safety habit

Your phone shows a preview of the link before opening it. Glance at it. If a code on a lamppost promises a parking meter but the URL looks like a random string of characters, don't tap — stickers over real codes are a known scam the FTC has warned about. Good to know

Do I need an app to scan QR codes?

No. iPhones and virtually all Android phones scan QR codes with the built-in camera. Third-party scanner apps are unnecessary and often full of ads. Apple's guide covers the Camera app and Code Scanner on iPhone.

How do I scan a QR code from a screenshot?

On iPhone, open the screenshot in Photos and long-press the code. On Android, open it in Google Photos and tap the Lens button.

Why won't my camera scan the QR code?

Usually the code is too small, low contrast, damaged, or there's no quiet zone around it. Wipe your lens, move closer, and see the full fix list in our troubleshooting guide.
