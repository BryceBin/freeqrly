Static vs dynamic QR codes

One of them is free forever. The other is a subscription. Here's how to tell which one you actually need.

October 13, 2026 · 4 min read

Every QR code generator wants to have the same conversation with you: have you considered dynamic? Before you answer, it helps to know what the two kinds actually are — and why the industry pushes one of them so hard.

The difference, without the sales pitch

A static QR code contains your destination directly. Scan it and your phone reads the WiFi password, the vCard, or the URL straight out of the pattern. No middleman, no server, no account.

A dynamic QR code contains a short link on the vendor's server. Scan it, your phone visits their server, and their server redirects you to the real destination. Because the destination lives on their server, the vendor can change it later — and count every scan.

That's the entire difference. Everything else is consequences.

When dynamic actually makes sense

  • You run campaigns. A poster goes to print next week but the landing page isn't final. Dynamic lets you point it somewhere now and fix it later.
  • You need scan analytics. How many people scanned, from where, on what. Static codes are silent; dynamic codes report home.
  • You A/B test destinations. Swap the link behind a printed code without reprinting.

When static wins

  • The destination won't change. WiFi passwords, business cards, app download links, restaurant menus, museum labels. Most QR codes in the wild are like this.
  • You don't want a subscription. Dynamic means paying forever — stop, and the codes die. Static is a one-time act.
  • Privacy matters. Every dynamic scan pings the vendor's server. Static scans touch nothing but the destination.
Here's the uncomfortable bit: most generators push dynamic because it's recurring revenue, not because you need it. If someone's "free" code requires an account, you're looking at a dynamic code with a trial timer.

A quick decision guide

  • WiFi for guests? Static.
  • Contact details on a business card? Static.
  • Menu that changes seasonally? Static — point it at your menu page and update the page, not the code.
  • Billboard for a product launch with unfinalized URLs? Dynamic — this is what it's for.
  • Need to report scan counts to a client? Dynamic.

Good to know

What is the difference between static and dynamic QR codes?

A static QR code encodes the destination directly in the pattern — free forever, uneditable. A dynamic QR code encodes a redirect on the vendor's server, so the destination can be changed later and scans can be tracked — but it requires an ongoing subscription.

Do I need a dynamic QR code?

Probably not. If your destination won't change — WiFi, business cards, fixed links — static is the right choice. You need dynamic only for editable destinations or scan analytics, typically in marketing campaigns.

Why do free QR code generators push dynamic codes?

Because dynamic codes require their server, which justifies a subscription. Many 'free' generators create dynamic codes tied to a trial — the code stops working when the trial ends.
