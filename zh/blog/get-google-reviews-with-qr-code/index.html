Get more Google reviews with a QR code

The best time to ask for a review is the moment a happy customer is standing in front of you. A QR code makes that moment one scan long.

October 19, 2026 · 4 min read

Google reviews decide who gets picked — and the businesses with the most reviews didn't get them by luck. They asked, at the right moment, with zero friction. A QR code pointing straight at your Google review form turns "please leave us a review" into a single scan.

Step 1 — get your Google review link

You need the direct link to your review form, not just your business listing:

  1. Go to your Google Business Profile and sign in.
  2. Open the "Reviews" section and look for "Get more reviews" — Google gives you a short shareable link (it looks like https://g.page/your-business/review).
  3. Copy it. That's the link your QR code will hold.

Step 2 — turn it into a QR code

粘贴链接 into a Google review QR code generator — it takes seconds, it's free, and the static code never expires. Print it at a decent size: business-card codes get ignored, but a 5 cm code on a table tent gets scanned. Keep dark ink on a light background with a clear quiet margin around the code.

Step 3 — put it where the moment happens

Placement is the whole game. The code has to be in front of a happy customer before they leave:

  • Restaurants & cafés: table tents and the receipt. "Enjoyed your meal? One scan." beats a follow-up email nobody opens.
  • Retail & salons: the checkout counter, right where they pay — satisfaction peaks at purchase.
  • Services (plumbers, tutors, clinics): a card handed over at the end of the visit, or a sticker on the invoice.
  • Delivery & takeaway: a sticker on the bag or box. The food is the reminder.

What to write next to it

A bare code gets fewer scans than a code with a reason. Keep it short and specific: "Love your visit? Scan to review us on Google — 30 seconds." Asking for Google reviews by name matters — customers default to whichever platform is easiest, and you want them on the one future customers check.

A few honest tips

  • Ask, don't beg. One visible code plus a spoken "we'd love a review" outperforms five signs.
  • Never pay for reviews or offer rewards for them — it violates Google's policies and the reviews get removed anyway.
  • Reply to reviews. A business that answers reviews gets more of them; reviewers can see you read them.
  • Track what works. If you want to know which placement drives scans, use a dynamic code for one location and compare — but for most shops, one static code that never expires is all you need.

小提示

How do I create a QR code for Google reviews?

Copy your Google review link from your Google Business Profile (Reviews → Get more reviews), paste it into a free QR code generator, and print the code. Static codes are free and never expire.

Where should I put my Google review QR code?

Wherever a satisfied customer stands before leaving: restaurant tables and receipts, retail checkout counters, service invoices, or delivery packaging.

Do Google review QR codes expire?

A static QR code never expires — it holds your review link directly. As long as your Google Business Profile exists, the code keeps working.

Should I offer a discount for a Google review?

No. Google prohibits incentivized reviews and removes them. Ask at the right moment instead — that's what the QR code is for.
